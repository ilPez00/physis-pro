## Result (trimmed)

```
C2 disc=-0.188 | held-out pi=0.271 M=0.028 | nulls=(-0.480,-0.016,-0.508)
   transfer=true specific=false heldout=false
C5 disc=0.498 | held-out pi=0.635 M=0.668 | nulls=(0.622,0.491,0.802)
   transfer=true specific=false heldout=false
VERDICT: FAILED — no candidate is arithmetic-specific against all nulls; no
candidate survives the held-out representations.
survivors: 0/5 | transfer true | specificity false | held-out false
```

- **4/5 candidates transfer** across ψ, θ — but **0/5 are arithmetic-specific**
  (the transferring structure is reproduced by the Cramér/synthetic nulls) and
  **0/5 survive held-out** (π, Mertens drift, C2 flips sign).
- This is E50's failure mode restated one abstraction level up: what transfers
  is generic cumulative-counting behaviour; what is prime-specific is locked to
  a single representation.

## What surprised me

- Cramér's 1/ln x model reproduces the *distributional* laws so closely that
  specificity fails everywhere. The arithmetic signal at the probability level
  is dominated by density, not structure — exactly the regime where naive gaps/
  return-time "laws" would be false positives.
