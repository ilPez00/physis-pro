

## 1. Truth Maintenance Systems — the ancestral core

This is the first thing I would study seriously.

**Jon Doyle, 1979 — Truth Maintenance System.** The original TMS explicitly maintains reasons for beliefs and revises them when discoveries contradict assumptions. It also includes dependency-directed backtracking and explanations. ([ScienceDirect][1])

[Doyle — “A Truth Maintenance System”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702%2879%2990008-0)

Then:

**Johan de Kleer, 1986 — Assumption-Based TMS (ATMS).**

This is particularly relevant to you because ATMS explicitly maintains **multiple alternative assumption sets**, allowing inconsistent information and simultaneous exploration of alternative solutions. ([ResearchGate][2])

[de Kleer — “An Assumption-Based TMS”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702%2886%2990080-9)

And:

[de Kleer — “Problem Solving with the ATMS”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702%2886%2990082-2)

**Why this matters to Physis:** your `Candidate / Supported / Contradicted / Failed / Certified` hypothesis machinery is essentially entering TMS territory. You should understand this lineage well enough that you can say exactly what Physis adds.

### Code you can steal ideas from

[ATMS-in-Python](https://github.com/FellnerDotDev/ATMS-in-Python)

It's explicitly an MIT-licensed Python ATMS implementation, although the author warns that it isn't complete. ([GitHub][3])

There's also:

[NATMS — Negation ATMS](https://github.com/Ruggiero-Santo/NATMS)

and:

[Truth-Maintenance-System implementation](https://github.com/satvikshetty04/Truth-Maintenance-System)

Those are useful as **small reference implementations**, rather than production dependencies.

# 2. Belief revision / AGM — extremely important for Physis

This is probably the second lineage you should absorb.

The question here is:

> If I learn `¬P`, what exactly happens to my existing belief state containing `P`?

AGM belief revision gives a formal framework for **expansion, contraction, and revision**.

There's actually a very recent 2026 survey explicitly tracing the lineage **from Doyle's TMS to AGM belief change**, which is almost exactly the historical bridge you need for your paper. ([Sage Journals][4])

[From Doyle to AGM: A Survey and an Implementation Roadmap for Belief Change](https://doi.org/10.1177/30504554261426170)

### And there is now a very relevant open-source project:

[Atlas — open-source AGM cognitive memory](https://github.com/RichSchefren/atlas)

This one made me sit up.

Atlas implements AGM-style belief revision on a property graph, maintains revision history, detects contradictions, and has a propagation mechanism called **Ripple** that re-evaluates downstream beliefs when an upstream belief changes. It claims 49/49 tested AGM scenarios. ([GitHub][5])

This is **very close to one slice of what you're building**.

Don't copy it blindly. **Study it and make it a baseline.**

In fact, I would explicitly run:

> Physis vs Atlas vs a vanilla vector store/RAG

on controlled belief-update tasks.

That would be excellent science.

# 3. Temporal Knowledge Graphs — another huge overlap

This is the modern lineage closest to your:

> “A thing can be true now, false later, and we shouldn't erase the historical state.”

The key concept is **temporal validity**.

Graphiti is probably the most immediately useful implementation:

[Graphiti GitHub](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti)

Graphiti maintains facts with temporal validity, provenance, incremental updates, learned/prescribed ontology, and hybrid semantic + graph retrieval. It explicitly invalidates old facts rather than simply deleting them. ([GitHub][6])

This overlaps strongly with your:

* temporal ledger
* provenance
* evolving ontology
* embeddings
* historical states
* incremental ingestion

And there is a substantial research literature around temporal knowledge graphs. A 2023 IJCAI survey is a good starting point. ([IJCAI][7])

[Temporal Knowledge Graph Completion: A Survey](https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2023/734)

There is also a 2024 survey specifically on **temporal knowledge graph embeddings**. ([ScienceDirect][8])

# 4. Provenance — quietly one of the most important pieces

Your append-only history has a deep connection to **data provenance**.

The foundational paper to know is:

**Green, Karvounarakis & Tannen, 2007 — Provenance Semirings.**

It formalizes how derived information can retain information about **where it came from and what inputs contributed to it**. ([DBLP][9])

[Provenance Semirings](https://doi.org/10.1145/1265530.1265535)

This gives you a much stronger theoretical vocabulary than simply calling your ledger “audit history.”

You can ask:

> What provenance information is necessary to reconstruct why a hypothesis exists?

That's a serious research question.

# 5. Paraconsistent reasoning — your contradiction system needs this lineage

You should investigate **paraconsistent logic**.

The fundamental idea is:

> `P` and `¬P` can coexist without the entire system becoming trivial.

That is extremely relevant to your insistence that contradiction should be **preserved rather than immediately resolved**.

Physis doesn't need to become a paraconsistent logic engine, but you should know this literature because otherwise reviewers can reasonably ask:

> “Why isn't this just paraconsistent knowledge representation?”

And you need a precise answer.

Your answer may eventually be:

> Because Physis represents contradiction not merely as a logical state, but as a temporally and empirically situated relationship between competing hypotheses.

That's potentially an important distinction.

# 6. Ontology + embeddings

Here your work is connected to **ontology embedding / knowledge graph embedding**.

There's a useful collection of implementations here:

[Machine Learning with Ontologies — implementations and resources](https://github.com/bio-ontology-research-group/machine-learning-with-ontologies)

It includes OWL2Vec, DL2Vec, RDF2Vec, Node2Vec, PyKEEN, OpenKE, etc. ([GitHub][10])

For Physis this is relevant to the question:

> Can your symbolic ontology scaffold and your continuous semantic space actually constrain each other productively?

That's much more interesting than simply asking whether cosine similarity works.

# 7. Modern agent memory

You should also inspect the current LLM-memory ecosystem, because **this is where people are independently rediscovering pieces of your problem**.

A very useful map is:

[Agent Memory Techniques — 30 runnable implementations](https://github.com/NirDiamant/Agent_Memory_Techniques)

It covers MemGPT/Letta, Mem0, Zep, Graphiti, episodic/semantic memory, knowledge graphs, etc. ([GitHub][11])

And notably:

[Nous — persistent epistemic substrate for AI](https://github.com/base76-research-lab/Nous)

Nous explicitly describes itself as a persistent epistemic substrate, with typed relations, confidence-aware retrieval, knowledge gaps, continuous learning, and graph-based memory. ([GitHub][12])

**That's worth studying carefully.**

Not because it invalidates Physis, but because it tells you what the modern AI community is converging toward.

# 8. The really interesting new project: Atlas

I'd give Atlas special attention.

Its architecture is approximately:

```text
facts
  ↓
belief graph
  ↓
dependencies
  ↓
revision
  ↓
Ripple
  ↓
downstream reassessment
  ↓
contradictions
  ↓
human adjudication
```

Physis is more like:

```text
observation
    ↓
embedding
    ↓
ontology / semantic classification
    ↓
hypotheses
    ↓
epistemic state
    ↓
outcome
    ↓
revision
    ↓
failure memory
    ↓
dream / replay
    ↓
new hypotheses
```

**That difference is exactly where I would concentrate your research.**

Atlas is primarily concerned with **belief dependency propagation**.

Physis is trying to make the **representation itself continuous and empirical**, and then attach belief maintenance around it.

That is a potentially meaningful distinction.

# What I would actually copy

Not code wholesale—**architectural patterns**.

### Copy/adapt from TMS/ATMS

* justification graphs
* assumption sets
* dependency-directed revision
* alternative worlds
* explanation generation

### From AGM

* revision/contraction/expansion formalism
* belief entrenchment
* formal revision invariants

### From Graphiti

* temporal edges
* validity intervals
* provenance
* incremental graph construction
* hybrid semantic/graph retrieval

### From provenance systems

* derivation lineage
* immutable evidence chains
* compositional provenance

### From paraconsistent systems

* contradiction without catastrophic collapse
* explicit `true / false / both / neither`-like states

### From ontology embedding

* graph ↔ vector mappings
* ontology-aware embedding
* semantic structure evaluation

### From Atlas

* downstream belief propagation
* revision cascades
* AGM compliance testing
* graph-native belief memory

### From agent-memory projects

* practical ingestion
* LLM interfaces
* episodic → semantic memory
* benchmarking infrastructure

# And I would build one monster benchmark

This is the part I'd recommend most strongly.

Create a common benchmark with:

```text
Observation stream
        ↓
    P1, P2, P3
     competing
    hypotheses
        ↓
   new evidence
        ↓
   contradiction
        ↓
    outcome
        ↓
    revision
        ↓
   later query
```

Then compare:

**A. Vector RAG**

**B. Graphiti**

**C. Atlas**

**D. TMS/ATMS**

**E. Physis**

And measure:

* contradiction retention
* stale-belief rate
* recovery after false evidence
* provenance completeness
* temporal query accuracy
* downstream revision accuracy
* hypothesis diversity
* outcome prediction
* catastrophic forgetting
* computational cost

That experiment would tell you **far more about Physis than another 50 pages of philosophical argument**.

## The research lineage I'd put on your wall

```text
                 SYMBOLIC EPISTEMOLOGY
                         │
             ┌───────────┴───────────┐
             │                       │
           TMS                     AGM
             │                       │
           ATMS              belief revision
             │                       │
             └──────────┬────────────┘
                        │
                 epistemic state
                        │
       ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
       │                │                │
   provenance      paraconsistency    temporal KG
       │                │                │
       └────────────────┼────────────────┘
                        │
                 knowledge graphs
                        │
                  embeddings
                        │
                  neural retrieval
                        │
                  agent memory
                        │
                        ▼
                     PHYSIS
```

And **this changes my assessment of the project slightly**.

The existence of all these lineages doesn't make Physis less interesting. It gives you a much clearer statement of what you need to contribute:

> **Physis is not inventing epistemic revision. It investigates an architecture in which continuous semantic representation, explicit competing hypotheses, contradiction-tolerant state, provenance, temporal validity, empirical outcome, and offline replay are coupled into one persistent runtime.**

That is a **much stronger research position** than “AI needs memory” or “RAG is bad.”

And I would absolutely use **Atlas + Graphiti + an ATMS implementation as your first three external baselines**.
