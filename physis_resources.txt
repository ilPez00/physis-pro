### Formal semantics track: Triune Continuum × Kripke × Physis

Investigate whether Physis can be formalized as an **empirically driven extension of a Tarskian/Triune semantic architecture by Kripke-style partial interpretation and fixed-point construction**. Naumenko's Triune Continuum Paradigm explicitly grounds system modeling in Tarski's theory of truth, Russellian type discipline, and the Triune Continuum; it was developed in an EPFL doctoral thesis and applied to RM-ODP/UML metamodeling.

**Research hypothesis:** Tarski supplies the grounding relation between domain/model and representation; Kripke supplies a formal model for progressively extending a partial interpretation; Physis adds **empirical outcome, temporal state, contradiction preservation, and revision** as constraints on successive semantic states. Do not claim that Physis is a Kripke truth theory: Kripke's construction concerns semantic truth/fixed points, whereas Physis concerns an open-world epistemic state.

A useful abstraction is:

$$
G:U\rightarrow M
$$

grounding observations/domain \(U\) into a model \(M\);

$$
\Gamma(I_t,O_t,V_t,C_t)\rightarrow I_{t+1}
$$

where \(\Gamma\) expands a partial interpretation using observations \(O\), outcome/verdict \(V\), and context \(C\);

$$
I_t\subseteq I_{t+1}
$$

for **additive semantic construction**, while revision/deprecation is represented as state/provenance rather than destructive erasure.

The important Physis departure is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{denotation}
\rightarrow
\text{representation}
\rightarrow
\text{semantic construction}
\rightarrow
\text{empirical constraint}
\rightarrow
\text{revised state}
}
$$

A “fixed point” should therefore mean **locally stable under currently available evidence/context**, not metaphysical truth. New observations may legitimately move the system out of that state.

### Formal implementation target

Represent each semantic object as:

$$
h=\langle e,\tau,\pi,\sigma,\rho,\kappa\rangle
$$

where \(e\)=representation, \(\tau\)=type/ontology position, \(\pi\)=provenance, \(\sigma\)=epistemic state, \(\rho\)=revision history, and \(\kappa\)=context/validity conditions.

Implement the semantic operator separately from the epistemic revision operator:

$$
I_{t+1}=\Gamma(I_t,O_t)
$$

$$
S_{t+1}=R(S_t,I_{t+1},V_t,C_t)
$$

This preserves the crucial distinction:

**semantic construction ≠ epistemic acceptance ≠ truth.**

### Exhaustive pseudocode sketch

```text
function PHYSIS_STEP(state S_t, observation O_t, outcome V_t, context C_t):

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 1. OBSERVATION / GROUNDING
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # O_t is an external observation, action result, document,
    # sensor event, human assertion, image, etc.
    #
    # Do NOT assume that the observation is already a proposition.
    # First construct a representation of what was encountered.
    e = EMBED(O_t)

    # Ground the observation against the current ontology/model.
    # This is the Tarskian/Triune-inspired grounding interface:
    # representation is related to a modeled domain rather than
    # treated as free-floating semantic similarity.
    candidates = CLASSIFY_AGAINST_ONTOLOGY(e, S_t.ontology)

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 2. PARTIAL INTERPRETATION
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # Construct only what is licensed by current evidence.
    # Unknown / undecidable material remains undefined.
    #
    # This is the Kripke-inspired principle:
    # do not force every proposition into TRUE/FALSE merely because
    # the system lacks sufficient information.
    I_partial = EXTEND_PARTIAL_INTERPRETATION(
        S_t.interpretation,
        candidates,
        O_t
    )

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 3. HYPOTHESIS GENERATION
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # Convert candidate interpretations into explicit hypotheses.
    # Multiple incompatible hypotheses may coexist.
    hypotheses = GENERATE_HYPOTHESES(
        I_partial,
        O_t,
        S_t.context
    )

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 4. SEMANTIC COHERENCE
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # Measure semantic/ontological compatibility.
    # IMPORTANT: this is NOT truth.
    for h in hypotheses:
        h.semantic_score = SEMANTIC_COHERENCE(h, S_t)
        h.ontology_score = ONTOLOGICAL_COHERENCE(h, S_t)

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 5. CONTRADICTION DETECTION
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # Detect conflicts without deleting either side.
    #
    # Contradiction is a relation in the state, not an instruction
    # to erase one proposition immediately.
    conflicts = FIND_CONTRADICTIONS(hypotheses, S_t)

    for (A, B) in conflicts:
        RECORD_CONTRADICTION(
            A,
            B,
            provenance = O_t,
            context = C_t,
            timestamp = NOW()
        )

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 6. EMPIRICAL / OPERATIONAL UPDATE
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # V_t records what happened when the relevant hypothesis/action
    # encountered the world.
    #
    # This is the specifically Physis layer:
    # semantic plausibility and empirical success remain separate.
    for h in hypotheses:
        h.empirical_score = UPDATE_EMPIRICAL_SUPPORT(
            h,
            V_t
        )

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 7. EPISTEMIC REVISION
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # Revise preference/status without destroying historical states.
    #
    # A failed hypothesis can remain historically important while
    # becoming dispreferred under the current context.
    S_next = REVISE_EPISTEMIC_STATE(
        S_t,
        hypotheses,
        conflicts,
        V_t,
        C_t
    )

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 8. TEMPORALIZATION
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # Attach validity intervals/context conditions.
    # A proposition can be preferred at t1 and superseded at t2
    # without retroactively becoming "never true."
    S_next = UPDATE_TEMPORAL_VALIDITY(
        S_next,
        V_t,
        C_t
    )

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 9. PROVENANCE
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # Preserve why every transition occurred.
    # The ledger is append-only; current preference is not history.
    S_next.ledger.append(
        EVENT(
            observation = O_t,
            interpretation = I_partial,
            hypotheses = hypotheses,
            conflicts = conflicts,
            outcome = V_t,
            context = C_t
        )
    )

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 10. PROVISIONAL FIXED-POINT TEST
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # "Fixed point" means that, under CURRENT information and
    # CURRENT transformation rules, no further revision occurs.
    #
    # It does NOT mean metaphysical truth.
    stable = EQUIVALENT_UNDER_CURRENT_EVIDENCE(S_t, S_next)

    if stable:
        S_next.status = "locally stable"
    else:
        S_next.status = "revised"

    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # 11. OFFLINE REPLAY / DREAM
    # ------------------------------------------------------------
    # Failed, inert, unresolved, or contradictory structures are
    # candidates for counterfactual exploration rather than deletion.
    #
    # This is where Physis can potentially extend the classical
    # semantic framework toward empirical hypothesis generation.
    queue = SELECT_REPLAY_CANDIDATES(S_next)

    for h in queue:
        proposal = COUNTERFACTUAL_REPLAY(h, S_next)
        S_next.proposals.append(proposal)

    return S_next
```

### Prior-art / resources to add

* **Triune Continuum Enterprise / Naumenko:** primary source for the paradigm and its Tarski/Russell foundations.
* **Doyle, “A Truth Maintenance System” (1979):** foundational computational belief revision/TMS lineage.
* **Almeida & Casals, “From Doyle to AGM” (2026):** particularly useful current survey connecting computational TMS history to formal belief revision.
* **Atlas:** open-source AGM implementation + dependency-propagating belief revision; use as an implementation/reference baseline, not as evidence of Physis novelty.

**Critical experiment:** implement the same belief-update benchmark under (1) monotonic/vector retrieval, (2) AGM revision, (3) TMS/ATMS, (4) Atlas/Ripple, and (5) Physis. Measure contradiction retention, downstream revision, temporal validity, provenance preservation, recovery after false evidence, and hypothesis generation. The scientific claim should be about the *additional behavior produced by coupling semantic representation to empirical epistemic revision*, not about inventing belief revision itself.


## 1. Truth Maintenance Systems — the ancestral core

This is the first thing I would study seriously.

**Jon Doyle, 1979 — Truth Maintenance System.** The original TMS explicitly maintains reasons for beliefs and revises them when discoveries contradict assumptions. It also includes dependency-directed backtracking and explanations. ([ScienceDirect][1])

[Doyle — “A Truth Maintenance System”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702%2879%2990008-0)

Then:

**Johan de Kleer, 1986 — Assumption-Based TMS (ATMS).**

This is particularly relevant to you because ATMS explicitly maintains **multiple alternative assumption sets**, allowing inconsistent information and simultaneous exploration of alternative solutions. ([ResearchGate][2])

[de Kleer — “An Assumption-Based TMS”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702%2886%2990080-9)

And:

[de Kleer — “Problem Solving with the ATMS”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702%2886%2990082-2)

**Why this matters to Physis:** your `Candidate / Supported / Contradicted / Failed / Certified` hypothesis machinery is essentially entering TMS territory. You should understand this lineage well enough that you can say exactly what Physis adds.

### Code you can steal ideas from

[ATMS-in-Python](https://github.com/FellnerDotDev/ATMS-in-Python)

It's explicitly an MIT-licensed Python ATMS implementation, although the author warns that it isn't complete. ([GitHub][3])

There's also:

[NATMS — Negation ATMS](https://github.com/Ruggiero-Santo/NATMS)

and:

[Truth-Maintenance-System implementation](https://github.com/satvikshetty04/Truth-Maintenance-System)

Those are useful as **small reference implementations**, rather than production dependencies.

# 2. Belief revision / AGM — extremely important for Physis

This is probably the second lineage you should absorb.

The question here is:

> If I learn `¬P`, what exactly happens to my existing belief state containing `P`?

AGM belief revision gives a formal framework for **expansion, contraction, and revision**.

There's actually a very recent 2026 survey explicitly tracing the lineage **from Doyle's TMS to AGM belief change**, which is almost exactly the historical bridge you need for your paper. ([Sage Journals][4])

[From Doyle to AGM: A Survey and an Implementation Roadmap for Belief Change](https://doi.org/10.1177/30504554261426170)

### And there is now a very relevant open-source project:

[Atlas — open-source AGM cognitive memory](https://github.com/RichSchefren/atlas)

This one made me sit up.

Atlas implements AGM-style belief revision on a property graph, maintains revision history, detects contradictions, and has a propagation mechanism called **Ripple** that re-evaluates downstream beliefs when an upstream belief changes. It claims 49/49 tested AGM scenarios. ([GitHub][5])

This is **very close to one slice of what you're building**.

Don't copy it blindly. **Study it and make it a baseline.**

In fact, I would explicitly run:

> Physis vs Atlas vs a vanilla vector store/RAG

on controlled belief-update tasks.

That would be excellent science.

# 3. Temporal Knowledge Graphs — another huge overlap

This is the modern lineage closest to your:

> “A thing can be true now, false later, and we shouldn't erase the historical state.”

The key concept is **temporal validity**.

Graphiti is probably the most immediately useful implementation:

[Graphiti GitHub](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti)

Graphiti maintains facts with temporal validity, provenance, incremental updates, learned/prescribed ontology, and hybrid semantic + graph retrieval. It explicitly invalidates old facts rather than simply deleting them. ([GitHub][6])

This overlaps strongly with your:

* temporal ledger
* provenance
* evolving ontology
* embeddings
* historical states
* incremental ingestion

And there is a substantial research literature around temporal knowledge graphs. A 2023 IJCAI survey is a good starting point. ([IJCAI][7])

[Temporal Knowledge Graph Completion: A Survey](https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2023/734)

There is also a 2024 survey specifically on **temporal knowledge graph embeddings**. ([ScienceDirect][8])

# 4. Provenance — quietly one of the most important pieces

Your append-only history has a deep connection to **data provenance**.

The foundational paper to know is:

**Green, Karvounarakis & Tannen, 2007 — Provenance Semirings.**

It formalizes how derived information can retain information about **where it came from and what inputs contributed to it**. ([DBLP][9])

[Provenance Semirings](https://doi.org/10.1145/1265530.1265535)

This gives you a much stronger theoretical vocabulary than simply calling your ledger “audit history.”

You can ask:

> What provenance information is necessary to reconstruct why a hypothesis exists?

That's a serious research question.

# 5. Paraconsistent reasoning — your contradiction system needs this lineage

You should investigate **paraconsistent logic**.

The fundamental idea is:

> `P` and `¬P` can coexist without the entire system becoming trivial.

That is extremely relevant to your insistence that contradiction should be **preserved rather than immediately resolved**.

Physis doesn't need to become a paraconsistent logic engine, but you should know this literature because otherwise reviewers can reasonably ask:

> “Why isn't this just paraconsistent knowledge representation?”

And you need a precise answer.

Your answer may eventually be:

> Because Physis represents contradiction not merely as a logical state, but as a temporally and empirically situated relationship between competing hypotheses.

That's potentially an important distinction.

It includes OWL2Vec, DL2Vec, RDF2Vec, Node2Vec, PyKEEN, OpenKE, etc. ([GitHub][10])

# 8. The really interesting new project: Atlas

I'd give Atlas special attention.

Its architecture is approximately:

```text
facts
  ↓
belief graph
  ↓
dependencies
  ↓
revision
  ↓
Ripple
  ↓
downstream reassessment
  ↓
contradictions
  ↓
human adjudication
```

Physis is more like:

```text
observation
    ↓
embedding
    ↓
ontology / semantic classification
    ↓
hypotheses
    ↓
epistemic state
    ↓
outcome
    ↓
revision
    ↓
failure memory
    ↓
dream / replay
    ↓
new hypotheses
```

**That difference is exactly where I would concentrate your research.**

Atlas is primarily concerned with **belief dependency propagation**.

Physis is trying to make the **representation itself continuous and empirical**, and then attach belief maintenance around it.

That is a potentially meaningful distinction.

```text
        proposal = COUNTERFACTUAL_REPLAY(h, S_next)
        S_next.proposals.append(proposal)

    return S_next
```
