# Product marketing — Physis Pro MVP

## Positioning

Physis Pro turns fragmented shop-floor facts into a shift decision:

> **Know where the shift lost money, what is drifting toward failure, and what
> the next shift should do.**

It is an industrial back-office intelligence layer for small and medium-sized
manufacturers that still reconcile machine events, scrap, and maintenance notes
in spreadsheets or at the morning meeting.

## First buyer

Start with the plant manager, production manager, or lean/maintenance lead at
an Italian discrete manufacturer with 5–50 machines. The economic champion is
the person accountable for throughput and downtime; the technical champion can
be an automation integrator or MES consultant.

## The wedge

Sell a fixed-scope **48-hour Shift Loss Pilot**:

1. Import one week of shift exports or enter one representative shift.
2. Compute availability, performance, quality, OEE, and the six-big-loss Pareto.
3. Establish a healthy baseline for one machine and flag drift/RUL where the
   data supports it.
4. Deliver an Italian HTML report and a 30-minute action review.

The call to action is not “buy AI.” It is “send one week of shift data and get
the loss review before your next operations meeting.”

## Proof points to demonstrate

- A bad OEE is decomposed into named losses, not just a red number.
- Scrap and downtime appear in the same shift narrative.
- Predictive maintenance is conditional on a baseline and exposes its sigma and
  data coverage rather than pretending to diagnose a machine.
- The report is printable, email-able, and written in the customer's language.

## Commercial message

Use service-led pricing first: €1,500–€3,000 for the pilot, then €500–€1,500
per plant per month for the reporting/monitoring service, with integration and
on-premise licensing quoted separately. Current OEE vendors commonly price by
machine/line and keep implementation/support in the commercial package; this
range is a starting hypothesis to validate in customer conversations, not a
promise.

## Product ladder

Physis should be sold in three steps, each with a concrete economic event:

1. **Review** — a paid Shift Loss or Admin Backlog Review produces a report and
   an exception queue from existing exports.
2. **Monitor** — recurring ingestion, baselines, reports, and human review keep
   the decision loop alive.
3. **Plan** — Decision Planner simulates ontology/process changes and shows the
   blast radius before a customer changes routing, metrics, or policy.

The third step is the defensible expansion. A competitor can copy a dashboard;
it is harder to copy a customer's history of meanings, corrections, evidence,
and counterfactual decisions.

## Value qualification

Before quoting, ask:

- Which recurring decision is currently made from a spreadsheet or meeting?
- How many hours or euros disappear when the decision is late or wrong?
- Can the customer provide one week of representative data?
- Who owns the decision and can approve a report or review queue?
- What evidence would make the customer continue for another month?

Do not quote based on document count or model usage alone. Quote against a
named workflow and the responsibility Physis is expected to carry.

## Sales sequence

- Build a list of 20 local manufacturers, lean consultants, and automation/MES
  integrators.
- Send a one-page Italian sample report with the demo link.
- Offer three pilot slots with a fixed deliverable and fixed price.
- Ask for the customer's export format and machine protocol only after the
  pilot is paid.
- Convert the report into a recurring plant license after the buyer uses it in
  two consecutive shift reviews.

Avoid leading with ontology, holarchy, dreams, or generic agent memory. Those
are differentiation and expansion surfaces after the buyer believes the loss
numbers.
