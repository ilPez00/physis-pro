# T — the target relational structure, stated before any run

The brief (§2) proposes a pattern: *entity → encounters other → interaction →
modification → differentiation → persistent identity → emergent order*, and the
claim that **interaction can produce and maintain difference rather than erase
it** (§4, §8).

Prose of that shape is unfalsifiable. What follows is the version this
experiment tests. It is fixed here, before any stimulus was shown to any model.

A system `S = <E, rho>` — two or more entities `E` under a relation `rho` —
**instantiates T** iff all five clauses hold:

| # | clause | holds iff |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **coupling** | each entity's trajectory depends on the other's; not common cause, not co-occurrence |
| C2 | **modification** | the coupling changes at least one entity's realised configuration relative to its uncoupled counterfactual |
| C3 | **invariance** | each entity retains an identity-constituting invariant across the modification: it stays individuable as the same kind of thing |
| C4 | **differentiation** | the modification *increases* distinguishability — between the entities, or of each from its uncoupled counterfactual |
| C5 | **emergent order** | an ensemble-level regularity holds that is not represented in any single entity's local rule |

The clauses are what makes the hypothesis refutable, because each one has a
natural negation that produces a system which *looks* like T and is not:

- `¬C1` — **common cause.** Stalactite and stalagmite grow toward each other; they
  do not interact. Ice-cream sales and drownings.
- `¬C2` — **no change.** Two gears mesh; neither is altered by meshing.
- `¬C3` — **identity destroyed.** Salt dissolves. Droplets coalesce. Two firms merge
  into one brand. Interaction here *does* eliminate difference — the case the
  brief claims is not the only one, which means it must be admitted as real.
- `¬C4` — **convergence.** Coupled pendulum clocks synchronise. Fireflies phase-lock.
  Dialects level under mass media. Maximal interaction, maximal emergent order,
  and difference *decreases*. This is the sharpest negation: it is the textbook
  emergence story, and T is not it.
- `¬C5` — **no ensemble regularity.** A bolt in a nut.

**The claim under test is therefore narrow and can lose.** It is not
"everything interacts" (§17's trivial universal). It is that T — coupling that
*increases* differentiation while preserving identity — is a real pattern with a
real complement, and that the complement is populated by exactly the phenomena
people reach for when they tell emergence stories.

## What "an abstraction is good" means here

Not that it reads well. An abstraction is good **iff it discriminates**: accepts
unseen systems that instantiate T and rejects the ones that violate a clause.
That is a measurable quantity (Cohen's kappa on a labelled probe set) and it is
the only quantity this experiment reports.

This choice has teeth. An abstraction can score *worse* than useless by being
more general — "two things interact and something emerges" accepts every probe
in the set, positive and negative alike, and earns kappa 0. §17 lists that
outcome ("trivial universal principles") as a failure mode of cross-domain
analogy; here it is the measured null, not a worry.
