## 1A — Holm

**HYPOTHESIS.** The multiplicity correction reported in P1 is computed and
explained incorrectly.

**KILL TEST.** Recompute from the stored p-values with an independent
implementation; if the rejection set changes, the manuscript's claim is wrong.

**NULL.** Not applicable — this is a correctness audit of an estimator.

**DATA.** `research/e1_multiplicity.json` (session-level bootstrap p-values,
10,000 replicates, 12 directional pairs).

**INFERENCE UNIT.** session.

**PROCEDURE.** Step-down Holm with `alpha/(m-r+1)`, an independently coded
adjusted-p formulation, Benjamini–Hochberg, and four simulation tests: FWER
under the global null (20,000 draws), planted-signal detection (2,000 draws),
cross-formulation agreement, and a frozen rejection set.

**RESULT.** The *value* in the manuscript was right (0.006); the *arithmetic
shown* was wrong. `cline→opencode` sits at rank 5 of 12, so the denominator is
`12-5+1 = 8` and the threshold is `0.05/8 = 0.00625`. The manuscript showed
`0.05/10`, which is rank 3's denominator.

**Root cause.** The v1 implementation `break`s out of the step-down loop at the
first non-rejection, leaving `thr` unset for every later hypothesis. Rejection
stops at rank 4, so ranks 5–12 carried `0.0`. Reading a threshold off a row the
procedure never visited is therefore structurally easy — which is how the
manuscript came to quote a denominator from the wrong rank. v2 fills every
threshold and tracks `stop_rank` separately.

**Tests.** FWER 0.0493 (alpha 0.05, 4se 0.0062) · planted-signal 1.0000 · both
Holm formulations agree on 3 rejections · BH (6) ⊇ Holm (3) · frozen set
`{claude-code→codex, codex→opencode, codex→cline}` matches.

**VERDICT.** Claim unchanged in substance: **no pair shows positive transfer
after family-wise correction**, and the three Holm survivors are all *below*
chance. Six pairs are **nominally significant**; three **survive Holm**. The
sentence is now generated by the script, and the registry validator fails the
build if `0.05/10` reappears.

**LIMITATION.** The p-values are bootstrap p-values with a floor at
`1/N_BOOT = 1e-4`; the three Holm survivors sit exactly at that floor, so their
individual p-values are censored and only their ordering is informative.

**REPRODUCTION.** `python3 research/papers/p1_multiplicity_v2.py`
**ARTIFACT.** `research/p1_multiplicity_v2.json`

## 1B — repeated partitions (opencode → claude-code)

**HYPOTHESIS.** The leak-free cold-start result ("borrowing never helps, local
data wins immediately") holds only for the single seeded 30/12 split.

**KILL TEST.** Repeat the partition 500 times. If the borrowed prior exceeds
chance in a substantial fraction of partitions, the claim is an artifact of one
split.

**NULL.** chance (AUC 0.5) and the borrowed-only arm. The global-base-rate and
chance arms requested are the *same quantity* — a constant predictor has AUC
exactly 0.5 — so one is reported, not two.

**DATA.** `benchmarks/behaviour/corpus-redacted.json.gz`; target claude-code
(42 sessions), source opencode, 500 partitions × 30 train / 12 test × 9 values
of N. 4,500 partition-N rows.

**INFERENCE UNIT.** session. No test session enters any prior.

**PROCEDURE.** Shuffle the 42 target sessions, take 30/12, sweep N over the
training pool, score every arm on the same held-out sessions. Summarise across
partitions.

**RESULT.**

| N | local | borrow | mixed | local>borrow | borrow>0.5 | mixed>local | coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.545 | 0.469 | 0.529 | 0.916 | 0.042 | 0.234 | 0.17 |
| 2 | 0.581 | 0.469 | 0.568 | 0.986 | 0.042 | 0.290 | 0.25 |
| 3 | 0.595 | 0.469 | 0.582 | 0.992 | 0.042 | 0.290 | 0.29 |
| 5 | 0.612 | 0.469 | 0.595 | 1.000 | 0.042 | 0.262 | 0.36 |
| 10 | 0.628 | 0.469 | 0.614 | 1.000 | 0.042 | 0.246 | 0.44 |
| 30 | 0.644 | 0.469 | 0.646 | 1.000 | 0.042 | 0.604 | 0.56 |

Borrowed is above chance in **4.2%** of partitions, flat across N — it is fixed
within a partition, so that fraction is a property of partitions, not history
size. Local beats borrowed in **≥99%** of partitions from N=3 and reliably
(≥95%) from **N=2**. Mixing **never consistently beats local** (max 60.4%).

**VERDICT.** Survives. For this pair the claim is safe.

**LIMITATION.** 4.2% of partitions are not zero. Those are partitions where the
borrowed prior happens to beat chance; the corpus cannot say why, and the rate
is low enough to be consistent with the prior being uninformative rather than
actively harmful in a minority of splits.

**REPRODUCTION.** `python3 research/papers/p1_repeated_splits.py --partitions 500`
**ARTIFACT.** `research/p1_repeated_splits.json` (5.4 MB, per-partition rows kept)

## 1C — all target × source pairs

**HYPOTHESIS.** "Borrowing never helps" is a property of cross-harness
transfer, not of one pair.

**KILL TEST.** Run the same leak-free experiment for every target large enough
to support a 30/12 split, against every source. If any pair benefits, the global
claim is refuted.

**NULL.** chance (AUC 0.5) per arm; the borrowed arm is its own reference.

**DATA.** Targets: claude-code (42), cline (69), opencode (288). **Codex (3) is
excluded as a target** — it cannot support a learning curve — but serves as a
source. 3 targets × 3 sources × 100 partitions × 9 values of N = 16,200 cells.

**INFERENCE UNIT.** session.

**PROCEDURE.** As 1B, for each (target, source) cell.

**RESULT.** Two of nine pairs show the borrowed prior above chance in a
majority of partitions:

| pair | borrowed above chance, max over N | local beats borrowed, min over N | mixing beats local, max |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cline←opencode** | **0.665** | 0.825 | 0.865 |
| **opencode←cline** | **0.635** | 0.645 | 0.645 |
| opencode←claude-code | 0.450 | 0.735 | 0.555 |
| cline←claude-code | 0.325 | 0.900 | 0.775 |
| claude-code←codex | 0.225 | 0.835 | 0.905 |
| opencode←codex | 0.190 | 0.875 | 0.515 |
| claude-code←cline | 0.095 | 0.885 | 0.670 |
| cline←codex | 0.070 | 0.970 | 0.585 |
| claude-code←opencode | 0.050 | 0.890 | 0.650 |

**`cline↔opencode` benefits in both directions.** Those are the same two
harnesses that the multiplicity analysis flagged: `cline→opencode` is the only
nominally-significant positive-direction transfer in the whole 12-pair family.

**VERDICT.** **The global claim is refuted and must be narrowed.** The correct
statement is pair-specific: *cross-harness borrowing helps when two harnesses
share failure structure — here cline and opencode, in both directions — and not
otherwise.* The 1B result is a property of the claude-code target, not of
cross-harness transfer in general.

**Note the tension with 1A, which is real and must be reported.** Under the
correct inferential unit and family-wise correction, `cline→opencode` does
**not** survive Holm (nominal p = 0.0094 vs a 0.00625 threshold). The cold-start
experiment says that pair benefits; the multiplicity analysis says its
significance is BH-dependent. Both are true and neither is decisive alone: the
multiplicity test asks whether a single AUC exceeds chance, while 1C asks
whether a *classifier built from that prior* beats chance across partitions.
They are different questions with different answers, and the paper should say
so rather than pick the convenient one.

**LIMITATION.** 100 partitions per cell, not 500. `cline` has 69 sessions and
`opencode` 288, so the train pools differ in size across targets, which may
contribute to the asymmetry. A source's *coverage* of the target's kinds is a
candidate explanation and is not yet tested as a predictor.

**REPRODUCTION.** `python3 research/papers/p1_all_targets_coldstart.py --partitions 100`
**ARTIFACT.** `research/p1_all_targets_coldstart.json`

## 1D — shrinkage prior

**HYPOTHESIS.** Naive transfer fails because it substitutes source rates for
target rates. As *shrinkage* the source prior may still carry usable signal, and
"cross-agent information contains no usable signal" is a stronger claim than
"naive transfer fails" — it is the one worth trying to kill.

**KILL TEST.** Shrink toward the SOURCE rate versus shrinking toward the
TARGET's own global rate, both with their own λ tuned on an inner training
split. If source-shrinkage does not beat global-shrinkage, the source adds
nothing that target-level shrinkage does not already supply.

**NULL.** Laplace smoothing `(f+1)/(n+2)` and the local empirical rate. Beating
Laplace is *not* sufficient evidence: Laplace shrinks toward 0.5, which the
target's own base rate also beats.

**DATA.** Same corpus. 3 targets × 150 partitions × 7 values of N, sources
cycled. λ ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100}, tuned on an inner fit/tune split
of the **training** pool only.

**INFERENCE UNIT.** session.

**PROCEDURE.** `p_target(k) = (fail_local(k) + λ·p_source(k)) / (n_local(k) + λ)`.

**RESULT.** The decisive contrast is **source-shrinkage versus
global-shrinkage**, both with their own tuned λ. Source-shrinkage does **not**
beat global-shrinkage consistently:

| N | source − global (median) | source beats global (frac of splits) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | +0.022 | 0.67 |
| 2 | +0.022 | 0.67 |
| 3 | +0.014 | 0.53 |
| 5 | +0.003 | 0.53 |
| 10 | +0.001 | 0.60 |
| 20 | +0.004 | 0.53 |
| 30 | **−0.001** | **0.40** |

At very small N the source adds a small positive increment (~+0.02 AUC, positive
in two-thirds of splits). By N=30 it is *negative* and below chance-level
consistency. Shrinkage toward the source does beat plain Laplace smoothing,
but so does shrinkage toward the target's own base rate — and once the two are
compared directly, the source's apparent advantage largely disappears.

**VERDICT.** **The strong claim survives.** Cross-agent information does not
carry usable signal beyond what the target can supply about itself. Naive
transfer fails, *and* the charitable re-use as shrinkage fails too, against the
right control. The apparent gain from beating Laplace is a property of
shrinkage, not of the source.

**LIMITATION — this run is underpowered and must be re-run.** The estimates
above come from **5 partitions**, because the box OOM-killed the 150-partition
runs twice (swap is 17 GiB / 17 GiB exhausted at the system level; both
attempts died before writing output). At 5 partitions the companion claim
`shrinkage_beats_laplace_at_every_N` reads *False*, whereas the earlier
150-partition run that completed read *True* — so the λ-selection instability
is real and 5 partitions cannot settle the magnitudes. The **direction** of the
source-vs-global result is stable across both (median ≤ +0.02 everywhere, and
negative at the largest N), which is why the qualitative verdict is stated, but
the numbers should not be quoted until a larger run completes. A 40-partition
re-run was started and is recorded in the artifact's own metadata.

**BLOCKER.** Memory. `research/p1_all_targets_coldstart.py` (16200 cells) and
`p1_shrinkage_prior.py` (150 partitions) both exceed what this box can run
alongside its other services. Reduced partition counts and
`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` are the workaround; the corpus and these experiments would
run comfortably on a box with headroom.

**REPRODUCTION.** `python3 research/papers/p1_shrinkage_prior.py --partitions 40`
**ARTIFACT.** `research/p1_shrinkage_prior.json` (records its own
`n_partitions`; check it before quoting any number above)

## What this does to the paper

1. **The headline must be narrowed from global to pair-specific.** "Borrowed
   priors do not help" is false as stated; the supportable version names the
   pair.
2. **1B's finding stays but is relabelled** — it is a statement about the
   claude-code target.
3. **The anti-prediction finding (1A) is unchanged and strengthened** as the
   only effect surviving family-wise correction.
4. **The tension between 1A and 1C must be stated in the paper**, not resolved
   by preference.
5. P1's abstract, results and conclusion need a rewrite once 1D lands.
